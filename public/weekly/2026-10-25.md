# New Acquisitions: The Week of October 19 – 25, 2026

*From the Library, with my compliments. A back issue for new readers*

Dear Reader,

If you have just joined us, welcome. This is one of a short series of back issues that will bring you up to date on how the Library grew before you arrived.

The week of October 19 belonged to Russia. At the time our scour followed a Russia thread every day, and this week it produced some of the best American writing about the country, from the age of Catherine the Great to the age of Lenin. Here are Francis Dana, the Puritan sent to St. Petersburg in 1780, and George Mifflin Dallas's diary from the court of Nicholas I. Here are Seward and the purchase of Alaska, and travellers by sleigh across Siberia, by steamer down to the Danube and under the midnight sun. And here is John Reed, who watched the Winter Palace fall and wrote *Ten Days That Shook the World* while it was still hot. The novelists came too, in Isabel Hapgood's fine American translations of Tolstoi, Gogol and Turgenieff.

The rest of the week was very American: Thoreau twice (*Walden*, and *Cape Cod* in Amelia Watson's lovely illustrated edition), Jewett's *Pointed Firs*, Twain's *Connecticut Yankee*, Howard Pyle's *Wonder Clock*, and Rockwell Kent's magnificent 1930 *Moby Dick*, one of the great illustrated books of the century. Science took us to the poles with Nansen and Byrd and up to the heavens with Newcomb and Hale, and it found time for ants, Mendel and the new chemistry.

Happy reading,
*Eden*

## New on the Shelves

*★ Russia marks additions to our continuing Russia shelf.*

### Law (15)

- **★ Russia: Joseph H. Choate, *The Two Hague Conferences*** (Princeton, 1913): the American delegate's own account of the 1899 and 1907 peace conferences, which the Tsar convened.
- **Andrew C. McLaughlin, *The Courts, the Constitution, and Parties*** (Chicago, 1912): a great constitutional historian on judicial review and the party system.
- **Roscoe Pound, *Criminal Justice in America*** (Holt, 1930): the Harvard dean's diagnosis of a system built for a rural nation and struggling in the cities.
- **John F. Dillon, *The Laws and Jurisprudence of England and America*** (Little, Brown, 1894): the Yale lectures of the leading American municipal-law jurist.
- **Hampton L. Carson, *The Supreme Court of the United States: Its History*** (Huber, 1891; 3 parts): the Court's centennial history, rich in portraits of the Justices.
- **★ Russia: Leo Pasvolsky & Harold G. Moulton, *Russian Debts and Russian Reconstruction*** (McGraw-Hill, 1924): the Brookings study of the tsarist debts the Bolsheviks repudiated.
- **Adams, Lodge, Young & Laughlin, *Essays in Anglo-Saxon Law*** (Little, Brown, 1876): Henry Adams's Harvard seminar on the courts, land and family law of the Anglo-Saxons.
- **Max Farrand, *The Framing of the Constitution of the United States*** (Yale, 1913): the classic short account of the 1787 Convention, by the editor of its records.
- **★ Russia: Eugene Schuyler, *American Diplomacy and the Furtherance of Commerce*** (Scribner, 1886): the diplomat and Tolstoi translator on how American diplomacy actually worked.
- **Rudolph von Jhering, *The Struggle for Law*** (Callaghan, 1879; 2nd ed. 1915): the famous lecture arguing that defending one's rights is a duty, not just a privilege.
- **Zechariah Chafee Jr., *Freedom of Speech*** (Harcourt, Brace and Howe, 1920): the book that shaped modern First Amendment law and Holmes's turn in *Abrams*.
- **★ Russia: W. P. Cresson, *Francis Dana: A Puritan Diplomat at the Court of Catherine the Great*** (Dial, 1930): America's first envoy to Russia, who spent two years in St. Petersburg and was never received.
- **Benjamin N. Cardozo, *The Paradoxes of Legal Science*** (Columbia, 1928): Cardozo's lectures on stability against change, and liberty against law.
- **★ Russia: Benjamin Platt Thomas, *Russo-American Relations, 1815–1867*** (Johns Hopkins, 1930): the scholarly backbone of the story, from the Treaty of 1824 to Alaska.
- **Jeremy Bentham, *Theory of Legislation*** (Trübner, 1876): utilitarian lawmaking in Hildreth's translation from Dumont's French.

### History (15)

- **★ Russia: Kellogg Durland, *The Red Reign*** (Century, 1907): an American reporter's year inside the 1905–06 revolution.
- **★ Russia: Eugene Schuyler, *Selected Essays, with a Memoir*** (Scribner, 1901): the essays of the first American to translate Turgenev and Tolstoi.
- **★ Russia: Arthur Ruhl, *White Nights and Other Russian Impressions*** (Scribner, 1917): Petrograd in the months before and during the February Revolution.
- **★ Russia: Richard J. Bush, *Reindeer, Dogs, and Snow-Shoes*** (Harper, 1871): Siberian travel in 1865–67 with the Russian–American telegraph expedition.
- **★ Russia: Francis B. Reeves, *Russia Then and Now, 1892–1917*** (Putnam, 1917): an American famine-relief mission of 1891–92, remembered on the eve of revolution.
- **★ Russia: Henry W. Elliott, *Our Arctic Province: Alaska and the Seal Islands*** (Scribner, 1886): Russian America after the purchase, with the author's own drawings.
- **★ Russia: *Diary of George Mifflin Dallas*** (Lippincott, 1892): the U.S. Minister at the court of Nicholas I in 1837–39, and later in London.
- **★ Russia: Frederic Bancroft, *The Life of William H. Seward*** (Harper, 1900; 2 vols.): the standard life of the man behind the Alaska purchase.
- **★ Russia: J. M. Buckley, *The Midnight Sun, the Tsar and the Nihilist*** (Lothrop, 1886): Norway, Sweden and Russia in the age of Nihilism.
- **★ Russia: John S. C. Abbott, *The Empire of Russia*** (Mason Brothers, 1859): a popular American history of Russia from Rurik to Nicholas I.
- **★ Russia: Charles Augustus Stoddard, *Across Russia, from the Baltic to the Danube*** (Scribner, 1891): a New York editor travelling from St. Petersburg to Odessa.
- **★ Russia: Edward Alsworth Ross, *Russia in Upheaval*** (Century, 1918): a sociologist's tour of revolutionary Russia in 1917.
- **★ Russia: Paul Milyoukov, *Russia and Its Crisis*** (Chicago, 1905): the Liberal leader's Chicago lectures on why autocracy had to yield.
- **★ Russia: John Reed, *Ten Days That Shook the World*** (1919; with Lenin's introduction): the October Revolution, seen from the Smolny and the streets.
- **★ Russia: Ruth Kedzie Wood, *The Tourist's Russia*** (Dodd, Mead, 1912): a pre-war guidebook to a country about to vanish.

### Literature (15)

- **★ Russia: William Lyon Phelps, *Essays on Russian Novelists*** (Macmillan, 1911): the Yale professor who introduced a generation of Americans to the Russian novel.
- **★ Russia: Oliver M. Sayler, *The Russian Theatre*** (Brentano's, 1922): Stanislavsky's Moscow Art Theatre and its rivals, with colour plates of stage designs.
- **Howard Pyle, *The Wonder Clock*** (Harper, 1888): twenty-four tales, one for each hour of the day, with Pyle's own drawings and his sister Katharine's verses.
- **★ Russia: Leo Wiener, *Anthology of Russian Literature*** (Putnam, 1902–03; 2 parts): the first big English anthology, from the chronicles to Gorky.
- **★ Russia: Pushkin, *Marie: A Story of Russian Love*** (McClurg): *The Captain's Daughter* in Marie de Zielinska's early American translation.
- **Henry David Thoreau, *Cape Cod*** (Houghton, Mifflin, 1896; 2 vols.): with Amelia M. Watson's watercolour decorations on nearly every page.
- **★ Russia: Tolstoi, *Childhood, Boyhood, Youth*** (Crowell, 1886): the autobiographical trilogy in Isabel Hapgood's translation.
- **Herman Melville, *Moby Dick*** (Random House, 1930): **Rockwell Kent**'s black-and-white drawings, which made Melville's whale an American icon.
- **Sarah Orne Jewett, *The Country of the Pointed Firs*** (Houghton, Mifflin, 1896): the Maine coast, and in Willa Cather's view one of three American books sure to last.
- **★ Russia: Gogol, *Dead Souls*** (Crowell, 1886; 2 vols.): *Tchitchikoff's Journeys*, Hapgood again.
- **Washington Irving, *The Alhambra*** (Macmillan, 1896): Granada's palace and its legends, with **Joseph Pennell**'s drawings.
- **Joel Chandler Harris, *Uncle Remus: His Songs and His Sayings*** (Appleton, 1895): the revised edition with **A. B. Frost**'s classic illustrations.
- **Mark Twain, *A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court*** (Webster, 1889): with **Dan Beard**'s satirical drawings, which Twain loved.
- **★ Russia: Turgenieff, *Memoirs of a Sportsman*** (Scribner, 1903): the sketches said to have helped end serfdom.
- **Henry D. Thoreau, *Walden*** (Houghton Mifflin, c.1910): the Riverside edition, edited by Francis H. Allen.

### Science (15)

- **Thomas Hunt Morgan, *The Physical Basis of Heredity*** (Lippincott, 1919): the fruit-fly room's chromosome theory, from the future Nobel laureate.
- **Richard E. Byrd, *Little America*** (Putnam, 1930): the first flight over the South Pole, with photographs and maps.
- **George Ellery Hale, *The New Heavens*** (Scribner, 1922): Mount Wilson's builder on the giant telescopes and what they were revealing.
- **William Morton Wheeler, *Ants*** (Columbia, 1910): the founding classic of myrmecology.
- **Simon Newcomb, *Popular Astronomy*** (Harper, 1878): the Victorian standard by America's greatest astronomer of the age.
- ***Letters of Asa Gray*** (ed. Jane Loring Gray; Houghton, Mifflin, 1893; 2 vols.): Darwin's American champion in his own letters.
- **J. J. Thomson, *Electricity and Matter*** (Yale, 1904): the discoverer of the electron on the structure of the atom.
- **Leonard Huxley, *Life and Letters of Thomas Henry Huxley*** (Appleton, 1900; 2 vols.): "Darwin's bulldog," by his son.
- **Fridtjof Nansen, *Farthest North*** (Harper, 1897; 2 vols.): the *Fram*'s drift across the Arctic and the dash north on skis.
- **William Bateson, *Mendel's Principles of Heredity*** (Cambridge, 1909): the man who named genetics, with Mendel's papers translated.
- **Matthew Fontaine Maury, *The Physical Geography of the Sea*** (Harper, 1858): the founding text of oceanography.
- **O. M. Mitchel, *The Planetary and Stellar Worlds*** (Baker & Scribner, 1848): the Cincinnati Observatory's founder lecturing to packed halls.
- **Hugh Miller, *The Old Red Sandstone*** (Gould and Lincoln, 1858): a stonemason geologist and his fossil fishes.
- **Josiah P. Cooke Jr., *The New Chemistry*** (Appleton, 1876): atoms and molecules for the general reader.
- **T. E. Thorpe, *Humphry Davy, Poet and Philosopher*** (1896): the chemist of the safety lamp, laughing gas and Coleridge's friendship.

There are two editions. The **Lakeside Press three-volume edition** (Chicago, 1930, limited to 1,000 copies in an aluminium slipcase) is the collector's Holy Grail.
